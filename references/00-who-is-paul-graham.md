# 00 · Who Paul Graham is

From his own bio page [bio] and his account of how Y Combinator started [ycstart, Mar 2012].

| When | What |
|---|---|
| 1995 | "He and Robert Morris started Viaweb, the first software as a service company." [bio] |
| 1998 | "Viaweb was acquired by Yahoo in 1998, where it became Yahoo Store." [bio] |
| 2001 | "He started publishing essays on paulgraham.com, which now gets around 25 million page views per year." [bio] |
| 2004 | Published *Hackers & Painters* (O'Reilly). [bio] |
| 11 Mar 2005 | "Jessica and I were walking home from dinner in Harvard Square." By the end of the walk they had decided to start an investment firm. [ycstart] |
| 2005 | "He and Jessica Livingston, Robert Morris, and Trevor Blackwell started Y Combinator, the first of a new type of startup incubator." [bio] "So YC started with $200k." [ycstart] |
| Since 2005 | "Y Combinator has funded over 3000 startups, including Airbnb, Dropbox, Stripe, and Reddit." [bio] (Figure as stated on his bio page when accessed.) |
| 2019 | "He published a new Lisp dialect written in itself called Bel." [bio] |

Education: "an AB from Cornell and a PhD in Computer Science from Harvard, and studied painting at RISD and the
Accademia di Belle Arti in Florence." [bio]

## The ideas YC was built on

> "Investors should be making more, smaller investments, they should be funding hackers instead of suits,
> they should be willing to fund younger founders, etc." [ycstart]

> "Initially we didn't have what turned out to be the most important idea: funding startups synchronously,
> instead of asynchronously as it had always been done before." [ycstart]

> "That's one of the reasons we especially like funding ideas that might be dismissed as "toys" — because YC
> itself was dismissed as one initially." [ycstart]

## Why his advice is worth reading

- He has watched thousands of startups up close: "Once you've advised 100 startups all doing different
  things, you rarely encounter problems you haven't seen before." [users, Sep 2022]
- His essays are free, dated and online. Over 220 are listed on paulgraham.com/articles.html.
- He says plainly where advice stops working: "there are exceptions to practically every rule."
  [X 2026-09-29](https://x.com/paulg/status/2104956189008150584)

## Read him with his own caveat

> "The reason founders don't listen to us is that they don't believe us. At least not till experience
> teaches them otherwise." [users]

Much of his advice is counterintuitive on purpose. The skill presents it with the source, so you can read
the reasoning yourself.
