## The ideas YC was built on

> "Investors should be making more, smaller investments, they should be funding hackers instead of suits,
> they should be willing to fund younger founders, etc." [ycstart]

The idea that mattered most came later: "funding startups synchronously, instead of asynchronously as it had
always been done before." [ycstart] And he notes YC likes funding ideas that might be dismissed as "toys"
because "YC itself was dismissed as one initially." [ycstart]

YC's motto came about a month after it started: "Make something people want." [good, Apr 2008] He wrote in
2008 that if he were choosing again, that's still the one he'd pick. [good]

## Why his advice is worth reading

- He has watched many startups up close: "Once you've advised 100 startups all doing different things, you
  rarely encounter problems you haven't seen before." [users, Sep 2022]
- His essays are free, dated and online. The essay index at paulgraham.com/articles.html listed 234 titles
  when we counted on 7 October 2026.
- He says plainly where advice stops working: "there are exceptions to practically every rule."
  [X 2026-09-29](https://x.com/paulg/status/2104956189008150584)

## How to read this playbook

**Our reading**, for weighing sources:

1. **Essays first.** Essays are where he gives the argument, the evidence and the caveats. Most chapters
   rest on them.
2. **Posts as confirmation.** His posts on X are short and often about one situation. We use them to show
   an idea is still current, or for advice the essays don't cover, and we say when a post is about one
   company.
3. **Dates matter.** Essays range from 2004 to 2025. Numbers (growth benchmarks, round sizes) are as of the
   essay's date. Where his view changed, the chapter says so.
4. **YC context.** Many essays describe YC's batch, its application or Demo Day at the time. Our reading:
   the principle often transfers, the process detail may not.

## Read him with his own caveat

> "The reason founders don't listen to us is that they don't believe us. At least not till experience
> teaches them otherwise." [users]

Much of his advice is counterintuitive on purpose. The skill presents it with the source, so you can read
the reasoning yourself.
