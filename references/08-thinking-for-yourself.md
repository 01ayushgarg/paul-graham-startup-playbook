## Keep your identity small

> "People can never have a fruitful argument about something that's part of their identity." [identity,
> Feb 2009]

> "The best plan is to let as few things into your identity as possible." [identity]

> "The more labels you have for yourself, the dumber they make you." [identity]

## What you can't say

> "Do you have any opinions that you would be reluctant to express in front of a group of your peers?"
> The Conformist Test. [say, Jan 2004]

> "If everything you believe is something you're supposed to believe, could that possibly be a coincidence?"
> [say]

> "In any competitive field, you can win big by seeing things that others daren't." [say]

> "Draw a sharp line between your thoughts and your speech. Inside your head, anything is allowed." [say]

## Disagree well: the hierarchy [disagree, Mar 2008]

| Level | Form |
|---|---|
| DH0 | Name-calling |
| DH1 | Ad hominem |
| DH2 | Responding to tone |
| DH3 | Contradiction |
| DH4 | Counterargument: "contradiction plus reasoning and/or evidence" |
| DH5 | Refutation: "you probably have to quote them" |
| DH6 | Refuting the central point |

> "A DH6 response might be unconvincing, but a DH2 or lower response is always unconvincing." [disagree]

## Judge early work by its potential

> "The right way to deal with new ideas is to treat them as a challenge to your imagination... to switch
> polarity entirely, from listing the reasons an idea won't work to trying to think of ways it could."
> [early, Oct 2020]

> "It can help if you focus less on where you are and more on the rate of change." [early]

**Checks to run:**
1. What do you believe about your market that most of your peers don't?
2. Which "label" are you protecting in this decision?
3. On the last criticism you got: what DH level was it, and what's the central point?
4. Listing why the idea fails is easy. List how it could work.
