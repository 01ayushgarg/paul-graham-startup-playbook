# 05 · Founders

## Relentlessly resourceful

> "A couple days ago I finally got being a good startup founder down to two words: relentlessly
> resourceful." [relres, Mar 2009]

> "Not merely relentless... In any interesting domain, the difficulties will be novel... you don't know
> whether you're about to plow through a block of foam or granite. So you have to be resourceful." [relres]

The opposite: "Hapless implies passivity. To be hapless is to be battered by circumstances — to let the
world have its way with you, instead of having your way with the world." [relres]

## Determination beats intelligence

> "We learned quickly that the most important predictor of success is determination. At first we thought
> it might be intelligence." [determination, Sep 2009]

> "So here in sum is how determination seems to work: it consists of willfulness balanced with discipline,
> aimed by ambition." [determination]

The melon-seed model: "We can imagine will and discipline as two fingers squeezing a slippery melon seed.
The harder they squeeze, the further the seed flies, but they must both squeeze equally or the seed spins
off sideways." [determination]

## The five qualities YC looks for [founders, Oct 2010]

1. **Determination.** "As long as you're over a certain threshold of intelligence, what matters most is
   determination."
2. **Flexibility.** "The best metaphor I've found for the combination of determination and flexibility you
   need is a running back."
3. **Imagination.** "In the startup world, most good ideas seem bad initially."
4. **Naughtiness.** "They delight in breaking rules, but not rules that matter." The interview question
   (from Sam Altman): "a time when they'd hacked something to their advantage."
5. **Friendship.** "Startups do to the relationship between the founders what a dog does to a sock: if it
   can be pulled apart, it will be."

And in 2024, the shortest version: "Good at building. Genuinely interested in the problem. Very determined.
Talks a lot to users." [X 2024-11-25](https://x.com/paulg/status/1861029051722326339)

## Persistent, not obstinate

> "The persistent are attached to the goal. The obstinate are attached to their ideas about how to reach
> it." [persistence, Jul 2024]

> "The persistent are like boats whose engines can't be throttled back. The obstinate are like boats whose
> rudders can't be turned." [persistence]

> "Resilience means setbacks can't change your morale, not that they can't change your mind." [persistence]

The test from [startupmistakes]: "If in each new idea you're able to re-use most of what you built for the
previous ones, then you're probably in a process that converges."

## Cofounders

> "Cofounders are for a startup what location is for real estate." [13sentences, Feb 2009]

> "What people wished they'd paid more attention to when choosing cofounders was character and commitment,
> not ability." [really, Oct 2009]

> "Empirically it seems to be hard to start a startup with just one founder." [founders] The reason: "Each
> thinks "I can't let my friends down."... it's missing when there's just one founder." [startupmistakes]

## What it's really like

> "Unconsciously, everyone expects a startup to be like a job, and that explains most of the surprises."
> [really]

> "If you start a startup, it will take over your life to a degree you cannot imagine." [before, Oct 2014]

> "The way to succeed in a startup is not to be an expert on startups, but to be an expert on your users and
> the problem you're solving for them." [before]

> "Starting a startup is where gaming the system stops working... Startups are as impersonal as physics."
> [before]

On age: "Starting a startup is like a brutally fast depth-first search. Most people should still be
searching breadth-first at 20." [before] And "it's on you to develop your mind the way college would have"
if you skip it. [X 2026-04-27](https://x.com/paulg/status/2048903314759196893)

**Checks to run on a founder (including yourself):**
1. Tell a story of a time you hacked something to your advantage.
2. Foam or granite: what did you do the last time a plan failed?
3. Which of your ideas are you attached to, versus the goal?
4. Would your cofounder relationship survive a year of bad news?
