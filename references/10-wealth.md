# 10 · Wealth

The core essay is *How to Make Wealth* [wealth, May 2004], with an update in *How People Get Rich Now*
[richnow, Apr 2021] and an earlier argument in *Hiring is Obsolete* [hiring, May 2005].

## What wealth is

"Wealth is not the same thing as money." "Wealth is stuff we want." [wealth] Because wealth can be created,
a startup doesn't take its share from anyone; he says that if you plan to start one, "you're planning to
disprove the Pie Fallacy." [wealth] Economically, a startup is "a way to compress your whole working life
into a few years." [wealth]

## The two things you need: measurement and leverage

> "To get rich you need to get yourself in a situation with two things, measurement and leverage." [wealth]

- **Measurement:** your contribution can be seen. In his account, the way to get it is to be part of a
  small group, where each person's work shows. [wealth]
- **Leverage:** your decisions have large effects, typically through technology. "A good hint to the
  presence of leverage is the possibility of failure." [wealth]
- So a safe job is a warning sign: if there's no danger, there's almost certainly no leverage. [wealth]

His 2005 version of measurement: a company that hires you is acting, economically, "as a proxy for the
customer", and you can instead be valued directly by users. "The market is a lot more discerning than any
employer." [hiring]

## Users are the proof

"Users are the only real proof that you've created wealth." [wealth] He suggests treating a startup "as an
optimization problem in which performance is measured by number of users." [wealth] And he warns about the
distribution: "If the mean is 30x, the median is probably zero." [wealth]

## How people get rich now (2021)

In *How People Get Rich Now* he looked at the Forbes 400 and found the new fortunes coming "Roughly 3/4 by
starting companies and 1/4 by investing." [richnow] The companies mostly win by better technology. Startups
are easier to start because they're cheaper, and the value of a company is "a function of its revenue and
its growth rate." [richnow]

## The synthesis (our reading)

Across these essays, his model has four parts, which connect to the rest of this playbook:

| Part | His idea | Where it shows up |
|---|---|---|
| Create, don't take | Wealth is what people want [wealth] | Chapter 02: make something people want |
| Be measured | Small groups, direct contact with users [wealth, hiring] | Chapter 03: the growth rate is the measurement |
| Have leverage | Technology and hard problems [wealth, richnow] | Chapter 01: schlep and ambitious ideas |
| Survive the variance | The median outcome is zero [wealth] | Chapter 04: default alive |

## How to apply (our suggestion)

1. **Name the wealth.** One sentence: what do users get that they want, and how much better off are they?
   If you can't say it, return to chapter 02.
2. **Check measurement.** Would the market notice if your team did 10x better work next quarter? If the
   answer depends on a manager or a partner's opinion, measurement is weak. [wealth, hiring]
3. **Check leverage.** List what scales without you: code, distribution, a technical edge. If the list is
   only "we work hard", leverage is weak. [wealth]
4. **Check the downside.** He says possible failure signals leverage. Our addition: make sure the failure is
   one you can survive (chapter 04).
5. **Pick the harder option when in doubt.** He suggests picking a hard problem and then, at every decision
   point, taking the harder choice. [wealth]

## Worked scenario (invented)

Two founders weigh two plans. Plan A: an agency building custom dashboards for clients, at $15k a project.
Plan B: a self-serve product that does the same thing for $200 a month.

- **Measurement:** A is judged by each client; B by the growth rate across all users. B is better measured.
- **Leverage:** A's revenue scales with hours; B's with users. B has leverage.
- **Possibility of failure:** A is safe and B could fail. By his test that's a hint B has the leverage.
- **Our reading:** B fits his model; A is what ramen profitability can turn into if you're not careful
  (chapter 04).

## Failure modes

- **Mistaking money for wealth:** raising money or reselling demand creates none. [wealth]
- **Safety as a goal:** no danger, no leverage. [wealth]
- **Reading the mean as the expectation:** the typical outcome is zero, so you need to be able to afford
  it. [wealth]
- **Old essay, new numbers:** *How to Make Wealth* is from 2004. Its examples date; the model is the part to
  use.

**Checks to run:**
1. Where is your measurement: would anyone know if you did 10x better work?
2. Where is your leverage: technology, a small team, a hard problem?
3. Is there a real chance of failure? If not, where's the upside? Can you survive the downside?
