# 10 · Wealth

> "Economically, you can think of a startup as a way to compress your whole working life into a few years."
> [wealth, May 2004]

> "Wealth is not the same thing as money." "Wealth is stuff we want." [wealth]

> "If you plan to start a startup, then whether you realize it or not, you're planning to disprove the Pie
> Fallacy." [wealth]

## Measurement and leverage

> "To get rich you need to get yourself in a situation with two things, measurement and leverage." [wealth]

> "A good hint to the presence of leverage is the possibility of failure." [wealth]

> "If you're in a job that feels safe, you are not going to get rich, because if there is no danger there is
> almost certainly no leverage." [wealth]

> "All you need to do is be part of a small group working on a hard problem." [wealth]

## Users are the proof

> "Users are the only real proof that you've created wealth." [wealth]

> "Treat a startup as an optimization problem in which performance is measured by number of users." [wealth]

> "Start by picking a hard problem, and then at every decision point, take the harder choice." [wealth]

> "If the mean is 30x, the median is probably zero." [wealth]

## How people get rich now [richnow, Apr 2021]

> "How are people making these new fortunes? Roughly 3/4 by starting companies and 1/4 by investing."

> "These are mostly companies that win by having better technology, rather than just a CEO who's really
> driven and good at making deals."

> "The value of a company is a function of its revenue and its growth rate."

> "But the main reason it's easier to start a startup now is that it's cheaper."

**Checks to run:**
1. Where is your measurement: would anyone know if you did 10x better work?
2. Where is your leverage: technology, a small team, a hard problem?
3. Is there a real chance of failure? If not, where's the upside?
