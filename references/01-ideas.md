# 01 · Startup ideas

Citations use the essay's slug: `[startupideas]` = paulgraham.com/startupideas.html. Full list with
titles and dates in `SOURCES.md`.

## Don't think up ideas. Notice problems.

> "The way to get startup ideas is not to try to think of startup ideas. It's to look for problems,
> preferably problems you have yourself." [startupideas, Nov 2012]

> "The verb you want to be using with respect to startup ideas is not "think up" but "notice."" [startupideas]

> "The very best startup ideas tend to have three things in common: they're something the founders
> themselves want, that they themselves can build, and that few others realize are worth doing."
> [startupideas]

Organic vs made-up: "There are two types of startup ideas: those that grow organically out of your own
life, and those that you decide, from afar, are going to be necessary to some class of users other than
you." [organic, Apr 2010] "The worst ideas we see at Y Combinator are from young founders making things
they think other people will want." [organic]

## The sitcom-idea trap

> "At YC we call these "made-up" or "sitcom" startup ideas... the writers would come up with an idea that
> sounded plausible, but was actually bad." [startupideas]

Why friends' feedback misleads: "They say "Yeah, maybe I could see using something like that."... Sum
that reaction across the entire population, and you have zero users." [startupideas]

## Dig a well, not a shallow hole

> "You can either build something a large number of people want a small amount, or something a small
> number of people want a large amount. Choose the latter." [startupideas]

> "When you have an idea for a startup, ask yourself: who wants this right now? Who wants this so much that
> they'll use it even when it's a crappy version one made by a two-person startup they've never heard of?
> If you can't answer that, the idea is probably bad." [startupideas]

But a well needs a way out: "Facebook was a good idea because it started with a small market there was a
fast path out of." [startupideas]

He still says it in 2026: "There has to be some subset of users who need what you're making so
desperately that they'll use it even if no one else is." [X 2026-05-25](https://x.com/paulg/status/2059011859953410286)

## Live in the future, build what's missing

> "Live in the future, then build what's missing." [startupideas]

> "Pay particular attention to things that chafe you... When something annoys you, it could be because
> you're living in the future." [startupideas]

> "The way to get new ideas is to notice anomalies: what seems strange, or missing, or broken?"
> [getideas, Jan 2023]

Self-tests:
- "What do you wish someone would make for you?" [organic]
- "What's missing or broken in your daily life?" [organic]
- "Why doesn't someone make x? If someone made x we'd buy it in a second." [startupideas]
- "What problem do I wish someone else would solve for me?" [schlep, Jan 2012]
- "Would you use this thing yourself, if you hadn't written it?" [startupideas, note 14]

## Good signs that look like bad signs

- **It looks like a toy.** "When something is described as a toy, that means it has everything an idea
  needs except being important." [startupideas] "When someone comes to us with something that users like
  but that we could envision forum trolls dismissing as a toy, it makes us especially likely to invest."
  [organic]
- **You feel late.** "Worrying that you're late is one of the signs of a good idea." [startupideas]
- **The market is crowded.** "You don't need to worry about entering a "crowded market" so long as you have
  a thesis about what everyone else in it is overlooking." [startupideas]
- **It's a schlep.** "Your unconscious won't even let you see ideas that involve painful schleps. That's
  schlep blindness." [schlep] Stripe is the example: everyone knew payments were painful, few would touch them.
- **It frightens you.** "The biggest startup ideas are terrifying." "The best ideas are just on the right
  side of impossible." [ambitious, Mar 2012]
- **A reasonable expert says something that sounds wrong.** "If the person proposing the idea is
  reasonable, then they know how implausible it sounds. And yet they're proposing it anyway. That suggests
  they know something you don't." [newideas, May 2021]

And the base rate, from Peter Thiel's Venn diagram as PG retells it: the sweet spot is the overlap of "seems
like a bad idea" and "is a good idea", but "the vast majority of ideas that seem bad are bad." [swan, Sep 2012]

## Big ideas: approach obliquely

> "Empirically, the way to do really big things seems to be to start with deceptively small things."
> [ambitious]

> "Don't make a direct frontal attack on it. Don't say, for example, that you're going to replace email...
> Just say you're building todo-list software." [ambitious]

> "Truly big ideas don't sound big initially." [X 2023-12-20](https://x.com/paulg/status/1737459460233155057)

> "The best way is to shrink the idea down to its essence, then ask how broadly that essential idea could be
> expanded." [X 2025-08-07](https://x.com/paulg/status/1953596993714180162)

## The idea will change, and that's the point

> "We've always encouraged founders to see a startup idea as a hypothesis rather than a blueprint."
> [really, Oct 2009]

> "One of the biggest popular misconceptions about startups is that founders have a brilliant idea, then
> implement it. In fact more often than not the biggest idea comes later."
> [X 2026-10-04](https://x.com/paulg/status/2106769829105717318)

**Checks to run on any idea:**
1. Who wants this right now, badly enough to use a crappy v1 from strangers?
2. Is it organic (from your own life) or made up?
3. Would you use it yourself if you hadn't built it?
4. Is the market a well, and is there a fast path out of it?
5. Which filter is hiding the better version: schlep, unsexy, or fear?
6. What is your thesis about what everyone else is overlooking?
