# 01 · Startup ideas

Citations use the essay's slug: `[startupideas]` = paulgraham.com/startupideas.html. Full list with
titles and dates in `SOURCES.md`. His view has been consistent from *Ideas for Startups* [ideas, Oct 2005]
to *How to Start Google* [google, Mar 2024].

## Don't think up ideas. Notice problems.

> "The way to get startup ideas is not to try to think of startup ideas. It's to look for problems,
> preferably problems you have yourself." [startupideas, Nov 2012]

The best ideas, he says, share three things: the founders want it, can build it, and few others see it's
worth doing. [startupideas] He contrasts ideas that "grow organically out of your own life" with ones you
decide, from afar, other people need. [organic, Apr 2010]

His 2005 heuristic is the same idea: "look at something people are trying to do, and figure out how to do
it in a way that doesn't suck." [start, Mar 2005] And his 2024 version, for teenagers: once you're good at a
technology, "you see dotted outlines around the things that are missing." [google] All you need in an idea
is "something your friends actually want." [google]

## An idea is a question

> "The initial idea is just a starting point... not a blueprint, but a question." [ideas]

Most startups end up nothing like their first idea [ideas], so a wrong question is fine as long as it's
"wrong in a way that leads to more ideas." [ideas] To generate good questions you need familiarity with new
technology and the right kind of friends to talk with. [ideas] His recipe, in the same essay: "finding the
problem intolerable and feeling it must be possible to solve it." [ideas] He made the same point in 2009,
calling an idea a hypothesis rather than a blueprint [really, Oct 2009], and again in a 2026 post saying the
biggest idea often comes later. [X 2026-10-04](https://x.com/paulg/status/2106769829105717318)

## The sitcom-idea trap

YC calls ideas that sound plausible but that no one urgently wants *made-up* or *sitcom* ideas. [startupideas] Friends'
politeness misleads: "They say "Yeah, maybe I could see using something like that."... Sum that reaction
across the entire population, and you have zero users." [startupideas]

## Dig a well, not a shallow hole

> "You can either build something a large number of people want a small amount, or something a small
> number of people want a large amount. Choose the latter." [startupideas]

The test: "who wants this right now? Who wants this so much that they'll use it even when it's a crappy
version one made by a two-person startup they've never heard of?" [startupideas] But a well needs a way
out, the way Facebook started small with "a fast path out." [startupideas]

## Live in the future, build what's missing

"Live in the future, then build what's missing." [startupideas] Things that annoy you may mean you're
already living in the future. [startupideas] In 2023 he put it as noticing anomalies: what seems strange,
missing or broken. [getideas, Jan 2023]

Self-tests:
- "What do you wish someone would make for you?" [organic]
- "What problem do I wish someone else would solve for me?" [schlep, Jan 2012]
- "Would you use this thing yourself, if you hadn't written it?" [startupideas, note 14]
- What's broken in something people already use? [ideas]
- What do people who aren't like you want from technology? [start]

## Good signs that look like bad signs

- **It looks like a toy.** A toy "has everything an idea needs except being important." [startupideas]
- **You feel late.** "Worrying that you're late is one of the signs of a good idea." [startupideas]
- **The market is crowded.** Fine, if you have a thesis about what everyone in it is overlooking.
  [startupideas]
- **It's a schlep.** "Your unconscious won't even let you see ideas that involve painful schleps. That's
  schlep blindness." [schlep] Stripe is his example.
- **It frightens you.** "The biggest startup ideas are terrifying." [ambitious, Mar 2012]
- **A reasonable expert says something that sounds wrong.** They know how implausible it sounds and propose
  it anyway, which "suggests they know something you don't." [newideas, May 2021]

The base rate, from Peter Thiel's Venn diagram as PG retells it: "the vast majority of ideas that seem bad
are bad." [swan, Sep 2012]

## Big ideas: approach obliquely

> "Empirically, the way to do really big things seems to be to start with deceptively small things."
> [ambitious]

Don't announce a frontal attack: don't say you'll replace email, say you're building todo-list software.
[ambitious] Making things cheaper or easier is one route; he calls cheapness "a subset of a more general
technique: making things easier." [ideas]

## How to apply (our suggestion)

A two-week process built on his advice. The schedule is ours.

1. **Days 1 to 10: keep a problem log.** Each time something annoys you or seems broken, write it down.
   [startupideas, getideas]
2. **Day 11: turn each problem into a question.** *Would people who X use something that Y?* (our phrasing of his idea-as-question) [ideas]
3. **Day 12: run the well test** on the top five. For each, name three people who want it right now.
   [startupideas]
4. **Day 13: check the filters.** Is a schlep, an unsexy market or fear hiding a bigger version?
   [schlep, ambitious]
5. **Day 14: pick one and build the smallest version** your friends would use. [google, ds]
6. **Run `templates/01-idea-test.md`** on it.

## Worked scenario (invented)

A developer's log keeps showing the same entry: chasing teammates for code review. As a question: *would
small teams use a bot that assigns and nags reviewers in Slack?* Three people she knows want it now; she'd
use it herself; the path out of the well is every team with a review queue. **Our reading:** a good test
candidate. Build it for her own team first.

## Failure modes

- **Sitcom ideas** that everyone says are nice. [startupideas]
- **Waiting for the perfect idea** instead of a good question. [ideas]
- **Building for others from afar.** [organic]
- **Ignoring the schlep** because it's boring. [schlep]

**Checks to run on any idea:**
1. Who wants this right now, badly enough to use a crappy v1 from strangers?
2. Is it organic (from your own life) or made up?
3. Would you use it yourself if you hadn't built it?
4. Is the market a well, and is there a fast path out of it?
5. Which filter is hiding the better version: schlep, unsexy, or fear?
6. What is your thesis about what everyone else is overlooking?
