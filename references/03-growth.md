# 03 · Growth

## A startup is a company designed to grow fast

> "A startup is a company designed to grow fast... The only essential thing is growth. Everything else we
> associate with startups follows from growth." [growth, Sep 2012]

> "You can use growth like a compass to make almost every decision you face." [growth]

## Know your number, as a rate

> "If there's one number every founder should always know, it's the company's growth rate." [growth]

> "What matters is not the absolute number of new customers, but the ratio of new customers to existing
> ones." [growth]

**Benchmarks:** "A good growth rate during YC is 5-7% a week. If you can hit 10% a week you're doing
exceptionally well. If you can only manage 1%, it's a sign you haven't yet figured out what you're doing."
[growth]

**What to measure:** "The best thing to measure the growth rate of is revenue. The next best, for startups
that aren't charging initially, is active users." [growth]

| Weekly growth | Yearly multiple [growth] |
|---|---|
| 1% | 1.7x |
| 2% | 2.8x |
| 5% | 12.6x |
| 7% | 33.7x |
| 10% | 142.0x |

From 100 users at 10% a week: "After a year you'll have 14,000 users, and after 2 years you'll have 2
million." [ds]

## Set a weekly target and let it decide

> "We usually advise startups to pick a growth rate they think they can hit, and then just try to hit it
> every week." [growth]

> "Anything that gets you the growth you need is ipso facto right." [growth] With an explicit exclusion:
> "trickery like buying users for more than their lifetime value, counting users as active when they're
> really not, bleeding out invites at a regularly increasing rate to manufacture a perfect growth curve"
> [growth, note 8].

> "You can use the need for growth as a form of evolutionary pressure." [growth]

> "I told them to optimize for growth rate and let that decide."
> [X 2026-08-21](https://x.com/paulg/status/2090644637971263640)

## Read your growth honestly

> "Churn is the worst reason to have slow growth... It means people are actually trying the product and
> deciding they don't like it." [X 2026-03-10](https://x.com/paulg/status/2031492573697560705)

> "Graph the growth rate of the number you care about instead of the number itself."
> [X 2026-03-19](https://x.com/paulg/status/2034756891818004629)

> "You make what you measure... put a big piece of paper on your wall and every day plot the number of
> users." (a principle PG credits to Joe Kraus) [13sentences, Feb 2009]

## Growth fixes the rest

> "No credential will get you more credibility with investors than growth."
> [X 2026-09-01](https://x.com/paulg/status/2094839060711747596)

> "If you have decent growth, you'll win in the end, no matter how obscure you are now. And if you don't,
> you need to fix something." [startuplessons, Apr 2006]

**Checks to run:**
1. What is your weekly growth rate, as a ratio, on revenue (or active users)?
2. What weekly target are you committed to? Did you hit it last week?
3. Is slow growth from not being known, from friction at sign-up, or from churn? Churn is the worst.
4. Is any of the growth "trickery" by his definition?
