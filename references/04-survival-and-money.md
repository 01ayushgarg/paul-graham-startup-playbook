# 04 · Survival: runway, ramen profitability, not dying

> "If you can just avoid dying, you get rich. That sounds like a joke, but it's actually a pretty good
> description of what happens in a typical startup." [die, Aug 2007]

## Default alive or default dead?

> "Assuming their expenses remain constant and their revenue growth is what it has been over the last
> several months, do they make it to profitability on the money they have left? Or to put it more
> dramatically, by default do they live or die?" [aord, Oct 2015]

> "Half the founders I talk to don't know whether they're default alive or default dead." [aord]

> "It's probably not that dangerous to start worrying too early that you're default dead, whereas it's very
> dangerous to start worrying too late." [aord]

Use `templates/02-default-alive-check.md`.

## The fatal pinch

> "The fatal pinch is default dead + slow growth + not enough time to fix it." [aord]

Why the second raise is harder, three forces: spending more than last time, investors' higher standards for
companies that already raised, and "The company is now starting to read as a failure." [pinch, Dec 2014]

> "Y Combinator tells founders who raise money to act as if it's the last they'll ever get." [pinch]

If you're already in it: "re-evaluate the probability of raising more money... the probability is zero."
Then "you can shut down the company, you can increase how much you make, and you can decrease how much you
spend." [pinch] Reframe sales from "do you want to buy our product?" to "what do you need that you'd pay a
lot for?" [pinch]

## Don't hire too fast

> "Hiring too fast is by far the biggest killer of startups that raise money." [aord]

> "In fact the large staffs of successful startups are probably more the effect of growth than the cause."
> [aord]

> "The biggest preventable cause of failure is spending too much money, by hiring too many people, during
> this period." [X 2022-07-24](https://x.com/paulg/status/1551276104056311809)

> "Be really cheap. This will save you from hiring too many people, from renting a fancy office, and from
> growing by buying users instead of making great things."
> [X 2019-08-11](https://x.com/paulg/status/1160445442464739328)

## Ramen profitable

> "Ramen profitable means a startup makes just enough to pay the founders' living expenses... the main
> importance of ramen profitability is that it buys you time." [ramenprofitable, Jul 2009]

Its four advantages [ramenprofitable]: better terms from investors; more attractive to them; morale ("Now
survival is the default, instead of dying"); and no fundraising distraction ("You're lucky if your
productivity is a third of what it was before").

The risk: "it might turn you into a consulting firm... ramen profitability is a trick for not dying en
route." [ramenprofitable]

## Morale is the real cause of death

> "When startups die, the official cause of death is always either running out of money or a critical
> founder bailing... But I think the underlying cause is usually that they've become demoralized." [die]

> "Startups rarely die in mid keystroke. So keep typing!" [die]

> "The number one thing not to do is other things. If you find yourself saying a sentence that ends with
> "but we're going to keep working on the startup," you are in big trouble." [die]

> "Deals fall through." [13sentences] "After the first 10 or so we learned to treat deals as background
> processes that we should ignore till they terminated." [13sentences]

**Checks to run:**
1. Default alive or default dead? Show the arithmetic.
2. Runway in months, at current burn.
3. Plan B written down: what you'd cut, and the date you'd switch. [aord]
4. Any hire planned before you know what the business is?
5. Any sentence that ends in "but we're going to keep working on the startup"?
