# Worked example · Office hours, start to finish

> ⚠️ **Illustrative.** "Tutorloop" is a **fictional startup** and every number is **invented** to show how
> the skill works. The principles and quotes are real and cited (see `SOURCES.md`).

## The founder's brief

> Tutorloop is an AI tutor for high-school maths. Two of us, ex-teachers. We launched 4 months ago, have 2,300
> sign-ups and 180 paying parents at $15/month ($2,700 MRR). We raised $400k and spend $45k a month. MRR has
> grown about 4% a month. We want to talk about raising our next round, and we're about to hire 3 engineers.

## The office-hours notes (the skill's output)

# Office hours: Tutorloop

**The real problem:** you came to talk about fundraising, but the company is default dead and you're about
to make it worse by hiring. · **Default:** dead.

The arithmetic (template 02): $400k in the bank, $45k a month out, $2,700 a month in, growing 4% a month.
Monthly net burn is about $42k, so cash runs out in month 10, about 9 months away. Revenue at 4% a month
would only be about $4,300 a month a year from now, and wouldn't reach $45k for about six years. PG says
half the founders he talks to don't know which they are. [aord]

## What I'd focus on this week

1. **Don't hire the 3 engineers.** He calls hiring too fast the biggest killer of startups that raise
   money, and reads big staffs as an effect of growth, not a cause. [aord]
2. **Find out why growth is 4% a month.** That's about 0.9% a week (1.04^(12/52) − 1). His 2012 benchmark
   was "a good growth rate during YC is 5-7% a week" [growth, Sep 2012], which is roughly 23% to 34% a
   month, and he said even 1% a week suggests you haven't figured out what you're doing. [growth] Separate
   sign-up friction from churn, which he calls the worst reason for slow growth. [X 2026-03-10] Talk to 10
   parents who cancelled this week.
3. **Find the well.** Who wants this *right now*? "Who wants this so much that they'll use it even when it's
   a crappy version one made by a two-person startup they've never heard of?" [startupideas] Maybe it's parents of kids with an exam in 6 weeks, not every
   parent. Test that group.

## The unscalable thing to do now

You're ex-teachers. Sit in on (or run) 20 live sessions yourselves this week, and hand-write follow-ups
to every parent. "I have never once seen a startup lured down a blind alley by trying too hard to make their
initial users happy." [ds]

## The number to watch

Weekly growth in paying parents, as a rate. Pick a target you can hit and "just try to hit it every week."
[growth]

## Hard questions you should be able to answer

- What have you learned from users in the last month? [users]
- Would you use this yourselves (or buy it for your own kid) if you hadn't built it? [startupideas]
- What's Plan B, written down, with the date you'd switch? [aord]

## On fundraising

Not now. "The time to raise money is not when you need it... It's when you can convince investors, and not
before." [convince] Default dead with slow growth and little time is his definition of the fatal pinch.
[aord] Fix growth first; growth is the credential. [X 2026-09-01]

## Where you might be the exception

If one segment (say, exam-crunch parents) is already growing 5%+ a week on its own, that's evidence the
well exists. Show that number and the conversation changes.
